# 01 — EWI Professional Method: How Working Analysts Apply Elliott Wave to Forecast the Next Wave

**Scope:** How professional Elliott Wave analysts — primarily from the Elliott Wave
International (EWI) school (Robert Prechter, Jeffrey Kennedy, Steven Hochberg, Jim
Martens) and the EWF school (elliottwave-forecast.com) — actually apply the Wave
Principle in real time to identify the current position and forecast the next wave.
This is the practitioner workflow, not the textbook axioms.

**Status:** Research reference. Not investment advice. Written 2026-06-12.

## 1. Methodology: The Real-Time Counting Workflow

### 1.1 Top-Down Multi-Degree Analysis

Every professional EWI analysis begins at the highest practical degree and works
downward. The operative rule is stated explicitly in Frost & Prechter (Ch. 1) and
confirmed in EWI's own free educational material and the EWF school:

> "You start counting from the monthly time-frame through the different frames
> until you reach the frame that you want to trade on."
> — EW Strategy Blog, "How to Count Elliott Waves Like a Pro"
> (https://ew-strategy.com/blog/how-to-count-elliott-waves-pro-method)

The hierarchy as EWF maps it to timeframes:

| Degree | Timeframe used |
|---|---|
| Grand Supercycle | Monthly / multi-century |
| Supercycle | Monthly / multi-decade |
| Cycle | Monthly / multi-year |
| Primary | Weekly |
| Intermediate | Daily |
| Minor | 4H |
| Minute | 1H |
| Minuette | 15m |

Sources:
- https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/labeling-wave-patterns/
- https://www.elliottwave.com/trader-training-and-tools/elliott-wave-educational-series/

**Why the hierarchy is non-negotiable in practice:** A count on the 1-hour chart
means nothing if it contradicts the daily structure. Pros never start on a lower
timeframe and attempt to infer the higher degree from it — they anchor the higher
degree first, then drill down. This is the single most important procedural discipline
separating professionals from amateurs.

### 1.2 Anchoring Degree at Known Major Turning Points

The first step on each new chart is to identify the most obvious structural pivots —
the stand-out peak and trough over the longest available history — and assign degree
from those. Frost & Prechter (Chapter on "Wave Degrees and Wave Notation") state:

> "The stock market is always somewhere in the basic five-wave pattern at the largest
> degree of trend."
> — Frost & Prechter, *Elliott Wave Principle*, 10th ed., p. 24
> PDF: https://www.investmenttheory.org/uploads/3/4/8/2/34825752/elliott-wave-principle.pdf

The practical consequence: a multi-decade all-time high or a generational crash low is
treated as the anchor point for the Cycle or Supercycle degree. Every sub-count is
nested inside that anchor. The analyst does NOT begin by trying to label the most
recent month; they begin by assigning the Grand Supercycle / Supercycle termini first.

For equities, the canonical EWI anchor is the 1932 low (Supercycle wave II bottom)
and 1974 (Cycle IV bottom). All subsequent Primary degree counts are nested inside
those confirmed termini. Kennedy in the Trader's Classroom confirms this approach:
identifying the "clearest and most prominent" structural highs/lows as degree anchors
before any sub-wave labeling begins.

Source:
- EWI Educational Series, "Introduction to the Wave Principle"
  https://www.elliottwave.com/free/introduction-to-the-wave-principle/

### 1.3 Multi-Timeframe Alignment Check

After the top-down count is established, the analyst confirms that sub-counts on lower
timeframes are consistent sub-divisions of the higher-degree structure. A five-wave
advance on the daily chart that occurs where the weekly chart shows a corrective wave
must be the A or C leg of that correction — it cannot be relabeled as a new Primary
impulse just because it looks good in isolation.

The EWF school codifies this into the "Right Side" tag — a directional stamp derived
from the higher-degree sequence that tells the trader whether to buy dips or sell
rallies. Any lower-timeframe setup that runs against the Right Side is not traded.

Source:
- https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/trading-elliott-wave-chart-right-side-tag/

### 1.4 The "Right Look" and Competing Counts

**The preferred count is the one that satisfies the largest number of guidelines.**
This is Frost & Prechter's own language for ranking counts, restated by Jim Martens
(40-year EWI veteran) in EWI's on-demand ranking course:

> "The analyst usually considers as 'preferred' the one that satisfies the largest
> number of guidelines. The top 'alternate' is the one that satisfies the next largest
> number of guidelines."
> — Frost & Prechter, *Elliott Wave Principle*, Ch. 2
> See also: https://www.elliottwave.com/videos/how-to-use-elliotts-rules-and-guidelines-to-label-charts-correctly/

The "right look" principle (Kennedy's phrase in the Trader's Classroom) captures this
subjectively: a valid count should produce a chart that looks balanced and proportional.
Wave 3 should visually dominate. Wave 4 should look like a rest, not a collapse.
Wave 5 should not wildly exceed wave 3 in length unless it is clearly extended. If the
count produces a chart where wave 3 is the smallest motive wave or where corrections
are obviously too deep or too shallow for the degree, the count is probably wrong even
if it doesn't technically violate the hard rules.

**Hard rules immediately eliminate competing counts.** Elliott's three rules —
wave 2 never beyond wave 1 origin, wave 3 never shortest, wave 4 never into wave 1
territory (in a non-diagonal) — are applied first. Any count that violates a rule is
eliminated. What remains is ranked by how many guidelines it satisfies.

**The alternate count is always maintained.** Professional analysts do not discard
alternate counts when the preferred count is in force. The alternate acts as a hedge:
if the market violates a key invalidation level (the price that would make the
preferred count impossible), the top alternate becomes the new preferred. Prechter:

> "Alternate counts are simply side roads that sometimes end up being the best path."
> — Frost & Prechter, *Elliott Wave Principle*

### 1.5 Kennedy's Channeling Confirmation Loop

Jeffrey Kennedy refined Frost & Prechter's channeling technique into a three-channel
confirmation loop that professionals use as real-time verification of the count. The
channels both confirm which wave is in progress and project the next wave's target.

**Three channels (Kennedy, Trader's Classroom; also reported by EW Strategy Blog):**

**Channel 1 — Base Channel:**
- Draw through the origin of wave 1 AND the end of wave 2.
- Place a parallel through the extreme of wave 1.
- **Significance:** Defines the trend. Price must hold above the lower boundary to
  confirm the impulse structure is valid. A corrective pattern that respects the
  lower base channel boundary (stays within it) is consistent with a corrective label
  at the next larger degree. Only after price moves through the upper boundary of the
  base channel is an impulsive count appropriate — this is when wave 3 begins.
  The base channel is Kennedy's most important channel: it distinguishes a trending
  move from a corrective one.

**Channel 2 — Acceleration Channel:**
- Draw through the extreme of wave 1 AND the extreme of wave 3.
- Place a parallel through the bottom of wave 2.
- **Significance:** Encompasses wave 3. Breaking below the lower boundary of the
  acceleration channel confirms wave 3 is complete and wave 4 is underway.
  This is the confirmation signal that wave 3 has ended — the single most important
  confirmation available during a trend.

**Channel 3 — Deceleration Channel (Final Channel):**
- After wave 4 completes: draw through the ends of wave 2 and wave 4.
- Place a parallel through the peak of wave 3.
- **Significance:** The upper parallel is the primary wave 5 target. When price
  touches or approaches the upper parallel, wave 5 may be complete. A "throw-over"
  (price briefly punching above the upper line before reversing) is a strong signal
  that wave 5 is exhausting. A "throw-under" (wave 5 failing to reach the upper line)
  signals a weak, truncated fifth.

## 2. Wave Identification as Practitioners Do It

### 2.1 Wave Personality — the Character Fingerprint

Professionals identify waves by their personality (Frost & Prechter, Ch. 2; Kennedy,
Trader's Classroom; EWI free resources):

| Wave | Character fingerprint | Why it's diagnostically useful |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tentative, overlooked. News still bearish. Looks like a bounce. Volume may not expand. Hard to identify in real time. | Low confidence at inception; often relabeled in hindsight. |
| 2 | Sharp, deep, scary. Feels like the prior trend has resumed. Rarely makes news. Deep retracement (50%–61.8% typical). | The panic retracement that shakes out weak hands. Use this to validate the wave 1 label retrospectively — if it looks like a genuine corrective counter-move, wave 1 was probably real. |
| 3 | Strongest and longest. Momentum peaks. Volume surges. News turns uniformly positive. Fundamentals confirmed. "Wonders to behold" (Prechter). The EWO / momentum oscillator peaks here. | Hardest to miss once underway. Momentum confirmation is decisive here. Professional analysts do not trade wave 3 aggressively until the EWO confirms the breakout (see §2.3). |
| 4 | Boring, sideways, corrective-looking. Holds above wave 1 high. Investors know the trend is up but hesitate. Often a flat, triangle, or combination. | Use alternation: if wave 2 was sharp/deep, wave 4 tends to be flat/shallow. Kennedy: wave 4 is an excellent setup for wave 5 entry. |
| 5 | Frothy, euphoric, but breadth diverges. RSI and EWO make lower highs vs wave 3. Volume often lower than wave 3. "Amateur enthusiasm" (Prechter). | EWO divergence is the professional's wave 5 identification tool. If the EWO is NOT making a lower high vs wave 3 peak, the count may not be at wave 5 yet. |
| A | Sells off from the high; many still expect the trend to resume. Often 5-sub-wave structure if part of a zigzag. | Sub-count of A (5 vs 3) is the critical classifier for the larger corrective type. |
| B | Counter-trend bounce; treacherous. Can make new highs in expanded flats/running triangles, luring in longs. Never exceeds wave A origin in a zigzag. | Professional rule: **do not trade wave B alone**. It is corrective in structure and unconfirmed in direction. |
| C | Devastating in bear markets; looks impulsive (5 sub-waves). Once C is clearly underway, analysts trade it aggressively. | C's 5-wave sub-structure is the professional confirmation that the correction is in its final phase. |

Sources:
- Frost & Prechter, *Elliott Wave Principle*, Ch. 2 (Wave Personality)
- EWI Waveopedia: https://www.elliottwave.com/waveopedia/wave-personality/
- EWF wave personality: https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/elliott-wave-personality-characheristics/
- Forex Training Group personality guide: https://forextraininggroup.com/characteristics-and-personalities-of-elliott-waves/

### 2.2 The Elliott Wave Oscillator (EWO) — Tom Joseph's Rules

The EWO is the difference between a 5-period SMA and a 35-period SMA applied to
closing prices (Tom Joseph, Advanced GET, 1990s; adopted as the standard momentum
confirmation tool for Elliott Wave by the professional community):

**Formula:** `EWO = SMA(close, 5) − SMA(close, 35)`

In Advanced GET's implementation: `(SMA(5) / SMA(35) − 1) × 100` (percentage
version), but the histogram shape is identical.

**The five canonical EWO rules (Tom Joseph / Advanced GET; confirmed across EWF,
EWI, MotiveWave documentation):**

**Rule 1 — Wave 3 produces the highest EWO peak (or deepest trough in a downtrend).**
If the program labels a wave as "wave 3," the EWO must exceed the breakout bands
(Advanced GET terminology) — i.e., it must reach the highest reading of the entire
impulse sequence. If the oscillator does not reach a new high during what is labeled
wave 3, the label is probably wrong and the count will be relabeled.
Source: https://kb.esignal.com/hc/en-us/articles/6362121282587-Advanced-GET-GET-Oscillator

**Rule 2 — Wave 4 retraces toward the zero line (94% rule).**
In 94% of cases, the EWO pulls back to the zero line (crosses or nearly crosses)
during wave 4. This zero-line pullback confirms wave 4 is underway and provides the
confirmation of the impulsive structure (waves 1-3 are complete; correction in progress).
A wave 4 that fails to pull the EWO back toward zero is suspect — it may be wave (iv)
of an extended wave 3 rather than a true wave 4.
Source: EWF oscillator description; Advanced GET reference guide
https://kb.esignal.com/hc/en-us/articles/6362141209755-Advanced-GET-Studies-GET-Oscillator

**Rule 3 — Wave 5 shows EWO divergence (lower high than wave 3 peak).**
When price makes a new high in wave 5, the EWO makes a lower high than at the wave 3
peak. This negative divergence is the primary mechanical confirmation that wave 5 is
in progress and likely near completion. If price makes a new all-time high but the EWO
makes a new high (not a lower high), the count is likely NOT at wave 5 — the prior
peak was not a completed wave 3 peak.
EWF confirmed this explicitly: "When Nasdaq reached all-time highs without momentum
divergence...it could be concluded that the previous all-time high was unlikely to be
wave V." https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/how-momentum-indicator-is-used-with-elliott-wave/

**Rule 4 — Corrective waves stay near or at zero, without strongly exceeding it.**
A healthy 3-wave correction (wave 2 or wave 4) will oscillate near or across the zero
line, but the EWO does not reach the high/low magnitude of the prior wave 3 peak.
Corrective waves lack the momentum to push the EWO strongly into positive/negative
territory unless an extended wave 3 is beginning within the correction.

**Rule 5 — Extended wave 5 uses a different oscillator setting (5,17 instead of 5,35).**
Tom Joseph's rule: for a standard 5-wave sequence, use the 5,35 EWO settings. If wave
5 is extending (moving well beyond 100% of wave 1 in length), switch to the 5,17
setting to capture the faster momentum dynamics of the extension.
Source: Advanced GET user guide (dl.farachart.com); eSignal knowledge base

**100–150 bar rule:** Tom Joseph specified that the EWO is most reliable when the chart
displays 100–150 bars — neither too few (noisy) nor too many (slow to respond).

Source for EWO section: https://tradingview.com/script/Y4MXHKKf-Tom-Joseph-MACD-5-35-for-Elliot-Waves/

### 2.3 RSI as a Structural Rule (EWF School)

EWF elevates RSI from a guideline to near-rule status:

> "Three RSI divergences are required for a valid 5-wave impulse."
> — EWF, "How Momentum Indicator (RSI) is Used with Elliott Wave"
> https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/how-momentum-indicator-is-used-with-elliott-wave/

The three divergences:
1. At wave (v) of wave 1 (sub-wave divergence within wave 1's own structure).
2. At wave (v) of wave 3 (sub-wave divergence within wave 3's structure).
3. At wave 5 itself vs wave 3 peak (the outer-degree divergence).

The clincher rule: if RSI makes a new high coincident with price making a new high,
that price high is NOT a completed wave 5 — the impulse is still in progress. This
rule is used directly to invalidate premature wave 5 labels.

**Corrective vs. impulsive RSI rule:** Impulse structures should end WITH RSI
divergence. Corrective structures should end WITHOUT RSI divergence. This asymmetry
helps distinguish a corrective C wave (can end without divergence) from a wave 5
(should have divergence).

Source: https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/how-momentum-indicator-is-used-with-elliott-wave/

### 2.4 Rules vs Guidelines in Practice

The three hard rules are mechanical kill switches — any violation eliminates the count.
Professional analysts apply them first, before checking guidelines.

Guidelines are probability filters applied to rank survivors. The most important in
professional practice, ordered by frequency of application:

1. **Alternation:** If wave 2 is sharp (zigzag), wave 4 is likely flat/sideways. The
   converse is used as a default assumption in real time. Kennedy uses this to anticipate
   the corrective form before it completes.
2. **Wave 3 extension (equity default):** In equity markets, the extension nearly always
   occurs in wave 3. This allows the analyst to assume an extended wave 3 as the working
   hypothesis until evidence contradicts it.
3. **Equality of waves 1 and 5:** When wave 3 is clearly extended (>1.618× wave 1),
   waves 1 and 5 tend toward equality. This is the primary wave 5 target projection in
   the absence of channeling information.
4. **Depth of correction:** Wave 2 retraces 50%–61.8% of wave 1 in the high-probability
   scenario. Wave 4 retraces 23.6%–38.2% of wave 3. Violations are legal but prompt
   re-examination.
5. **"Right look" proportion:** A five-wave impulse should be visually proportional.
   Wave 3 should visually dominate. If it does not, the count is likely wrong even if
   technically valid.

Sources:
- Frost & Prechter, *Elliott Wave Principle* (guidelines throughout Chs. 1–2)
- EWI guidelines ranking: https://www.elliottwave.com/videos/how-to-use-elliotts-rules-and-guidelines-to-label-charts-correctly/

## 3. How Professionals Predict the Next Wave

### 3.1 Direction: What Follows What

The wave position completely determines the directional bias for the next wave:

| Current wave | Next wave direction | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| Wave 1 complete | Wave 2 down (correction) | Deep counter-move, often 50%–61.8% retrace. Scary — looks like a trend reversal. |
| Wave 2 complete | Wave 3 up (impulse) | Strongest move of the sequence. The professional's highest-confidence long. |
| Wave 3 complete | Wave 4 down (correction) | Sideways / shallow correction. Alternation with wave 2 determines form. |
| Wave 4 complete | Wave 5 up (impulse) | Final push. Weaker than wave 3. Watch for EWO divergence and RSI divergence at completion. |
| Wave 5 complete | Corrective sequence A-B-C or WXY | The entire prior impulse will be retraced at minimum 38.2%; typically 50%–61.8%. |
| Wave A complete | Wave B up (counter-trend bounce) | 50%–61.8% retrace of wave A in a zigzag; can exceed wave A origin in an expanded flat. Do not trade. |
| Wave B complete | Wave C down (impulse in correction direction) | Equals wave A in length (zigzag: typical); extends to 1.236×–1.618× wave A (expanded). |
| Triangle wave E complete | Post-triangle thrust | 75%–125% of the widest triangle leg, in the prior trend direction. |

Sources:
- Frost & Prechter, *Elliott Wave Principle*
- EWF: https://elliottwave-forecast.com/trading/elliott-wave-corrective-waves/

### 3.2 Fibonacci Targets — Per-Wave Rules (Practitioner Table)

Professional analysts compute multiple Fibonacci targets per wave and look for
confluence. The primary target is acted upon; the secondary targets are contingency
levels.

**Wave 2 retracement targets (measured from end of wave 1 back toward wave 1 origin):**

| Priority | Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | 61.8% of wave 1 | Most common; the "golden ratio" retracement |
| Primary | 50.0% of wave 1 | Near-equal in frequency |
| Secondary | 76.4% of wave 1 | Deep wave 2; valid but rare |
| Secondary | 85.4% of wave 1 | EWF's "deep retracement" level; near-invalidation |
| Stop anchor | 100.0% of wave 1 | Rule: wave 2 must not exceed the origin of wave 1 |

Sources: EWF Fibonacci guide; Frost & Prechter Ch. 1
- https://elliottwave-forecast.com/trading/fibonacci-retracement-elliott-wave-guide/

**Wave 3 extension targets (measured from end of wave 2, projected in trend direction):**

| Priority | Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | 1.618× wave 1 | Canonical Fibonacci; "normal" wave 3 |
| Secondary | 2.618× wave 1 | Extended; typical for commodity markets |
| Secondary | 3.618× wave 1 | Strongly extended; equity bull markets |
| Rare | 4.236× wave 1 | Very extended; flag and measure check |

EWF convention: wave 3 minimum is 1.618× wave 1. A wave 3 less than 1.618× wave 1
is sub-standard and prompts examination for alternative counts.
- https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/elliott-wave-extensions/

**Wave 4 retracement targets (measured from end of wave 3 back toward wave 3 origin):**

| Priority | Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | 38.2% of wave 3 | Most common; consistent with alternation if wave 2 was deep |
| Primary | 23.6% of wave 3 | Shallow; typical in very strong trends |
| Secondary | 50.0% of wave 3 | Outer boundary; more common in weaker trends |
| EWF note | 14.6% of wave 3 | EWF's minimum "meaningful" wave 4 retracement |

Hard constraint: wave 4 extreme must stay above the top of wave 1.
Sources: EWF; Frost & Prechter; https://elliottwave-forecast.com/trading/fibonacci-retracement-elliott-wave-guide/

**Wave 5 projection targets (measured from end of wave 4 in trend direction):**

| Priority | Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Equal to wave 1 in log-price terms | Equality guideline; most common when wave 3 extends |
| Primary | 0.618× wave 3 length | When wave 3 is extended |
| Secondary | 1.618× wave 1 | Extended fifth |
| Secondary | 61.8% of total move (waves 1–3) | EWF's alternative: W5 = 0.618× (W1+W2+W3+W4 range) |
| Channel target | Upper 2-4 channel line | Kennedy's final channel upper parallel |

### 3.3 Invalidation Levels — the Professional's Stop Anchor

For every active wave count, the analyst identifies the specific price level that, if
breached, makes the count impossible under Elliott rules. This is the
**structural invalidation level**, and it serves as the logical stop-loss anchor:

| Active count | Invalidation level |
|---|---|
| In wave 2 (long) | Origin of wave 1 (100% retrace of wave 1) |
| In wave 3 (long) | Below the low of wave 2 (wave 3 cannot be the shortest if already past wave 1 length; technically the invalidation is a structural one depending on context) |
| In wave 4 (long entry anticipating wave 5) | Top of wave 1 (wave 4 must not enter wave 1 territory) |
| In wave 5 (long; taking late entry) | Low of wave 4 |
| In corrective wave Y of WXY (long) | 161.8% extension of wave W from end of wave X (EWF Blue Box lower boundary) |
| Preferred count vs alternate | The price level that, if breached, makes the preferred count structurally impossible |

EWF specific: "Stop losses should always be based on technical invalidation levels
rather than random price distances."
- https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/where-to-place-stop-losses-using-the-elliott-wave-principle/

Kennedy's teaching: when a specific wave count has an invalidation level, that price
doubles as the protective stop. If wave 2 cannot retrace more than 100% of wave 1,
placing a stop just below the wave 1 origin is the structurally-anchored stop.

### 3.4 Channeling as Real-Time Projection

The 2-4 deceleration channel (Kennedy's "final channel") is the primary wave 5 target
tool. Construction (after wave 4 completes):

1. Connect the bottom of wave 2 and the bottom of wave 4 with a line.
2. Draw a parallel through the top of wave 3.
3. The upper parallel is the projected wave 5 endpoint.
4. If waves 1 and 3 are "normal" (not heavily extended), the upper parallel touching
   the wave 3 peak gives the best forecast.
5. If wave 3 is very steep (nearly vertical), the lower line from waves 2 and 4 gives
   a better projection anchor.

Frost & Prechter: "If waves one and three are normal, the upper parallel most
accurately forecasts the end of wave five when drawn touching the peak of wave three."
Source: https://marscapitalpartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/01/EW-Basics.pdf

Kennedy also emphasizes: the logarithmic scale should be used for multi-year or
multi-decade charts (Prechter confirmed this for any instrument whose range exceeds ~2×).
Log-scale channels produce better wave proportion and more reliable parallel lines.

## 4. What Professionals Do That Naive/Automated EW Does Not

### 4.1 Top-Down Degree Anchoring (Automated Systems Skip This)

An automated counter starts at the most recent data and labels the nearest recognizable
pattern. A professional starts at the all-time high/low and assigns degree top-down.
The practical result: the automated counter frequently labels a multi-year move as
"Minute" degree because the pivot filter found a pattern within the recent price window,
while the professional's count correctly places the same move at Primary or Cycle degree
because it is anchored to the 1932/1974/2009 turning-point hierarchy.

This is precisely the failure documented in our engine's audit (R4, 00_MISTAKES_AND_FIXES.md):
"Degree computed on the partial node + ladder capped at INTERMEDIATE."

### 4.2 The Alternate-Count Discipline

Professionals never hold a single preferred count without maintaining at least one
alternate. In real-time service (EWI's Financial Forecast Service, Kennedy's
Trader's Classroom), every published chart carries a preferred count AND labeled
alternates with explicit invalidation conditions. An automated system that outputs
only the top-ranked candidate is missing the alternate-count infrastructure that
makes the professional system actionable.

The current engine surfaces alternate candidates via `label_and_validate`'s ranked
list, but does not associate them with explicit invalidation conditions or present
them with confidence scores anchored to rule violations (it uses a scoring heuristic
that reflects goodness-of-fit, not structural impossibility).

### 4.3 The EWO as a Hard Structural Gate

Professionals use the EWO as a near-rule confirmation gate, not just a supporting
indicator. If the EWO does not exceed its breakout bands during what is labeled wave 3,
the label is presumed wrong. If wave 5 does not show EWO divergence, the wave 5 label
is suspect.

Our engine has no EWO equivalent connected to the wave labeler. The `confluence.py`
RSI divergence strand partially implements the wave 5 divergence rule, but it is not
a gate on the wave 3 label (our engine does not require the EWO to peak at wave 3).
This means our wave 3 labels are structurally unconfirmed by momentum.

### 4.4 The Log-Price Measurement Discipline

Professionals working on multi-year charts use log-scale price measurement for all
Fibonacci and proportion calculations. Kennedy and Prechter both confirm that linear
price measurement is unreliable when the instrument has moved more than ~2× from its
base.

Our engine's most severe root cause (R1, 00_MISTAKES_AND_FIXES.md) is exactly this
failure: "Every wave-proportion comparison in the engine uses linear price differences.
Elliott/NeoWave require LOGARITHMIC measurement for any instrument whose range exceeds
~2×. AVGO spans 330×, MRVL 105×." This single error is why our engine cannot see
valid impulse proportions and labels everything as corrective.

### 4.5 The "Trade the Count You Can Act On" Rule

Professional EW traders are selective. Kennedy's explicit teaching: there are four
positions within a five-wave sequence that offer high-confidence setups:
(1) the start of wave 3 (after wave 2 completes), (2) the start of wave 5 (after wave
4 completes), (3) the start of wave C (after wave B completes in a correction),
and (4) the post-diagonal fast reversal (after an ending diagonal completes).

What professionals explicitly avoid:
- **Wave B**: "treacherous" (Prechter); corrective in structure, unconfirmed in
  direction, prone to failed labels. Do not trade standalone.
- **Wave 5 late entry**: "when prices are advancing in wave 5... and wave 5 has
  already completed three or four smaller waves, a trader knows this is not the time
  to add long positions." (Kennedy / EWI FAQ)
  https://www.elliottwave.com/trading/how-can-the-elliott-wave-principle-improve-my-trading
- **Truncated fifth waves**: EWF explicitly does not label truncated fifths — they
  seek alternative counts instead.

Our automated engine currently outputs the highest-confidence count regardless of
wave position. It does not distinguish high-confidence setups (wave 3 start, wave C
after wave B) from low-confidence positions (wave B, late-stage wave 5). This
"position agnosticism" is a gap relative to how professionals select trades.

## 6. Gap to Our Engine: What the Professional Workflow Does That We Don't

### G1 — No Top-Down Degree Anchoring (Critical)

Our engine (`automation.py`, `wavetree.py`) starts from the pivot stream presented to
it without any knowledge of the Grand Supercycle or Supercycle degree. It counts the
pattern visible in the window, not the pattern anchored to historical turning points.
For AVGO and MRVL, this produces Minuette labels on 16-year advances (audit R4). The
professional workflow's anchor step is entirely absent.

**Gap fix direction:** Implement an explicit "degree anchor" input: the user provides
the highest-degree turning points (e.g., AVGO 2009 low at $2.64 = Cycle degree II
bottom), and all sub-counts are constrained to be consistent sub-divisions of those
anchors. This is not automatable from price data alone — it requires a one-time human
input per instrument, exactly as professionals provide it.

### G2 — EWO Confirmation Not Wired Into the Wave Labeler (High)

The engine labels wave 3 without requiring that the EWO exceeded its highest value
at that peak. Tom Joseph's rule (wave 3 must produce the highest EWO reading) is a
near-rule confirmation gate that professional systems use to reject spurious wave 3
labels. Our `confluence.py` has RSI divergence for wave 5, but has no EWO-equivalent
gate on the wave 3 label. Adding an EWO (SMA-5 minus SMA-35) check — requiring the
EWO histogram to show its highest value at the labeled wave 3 extreme — would
significantly reduce false wave 3 labels.

**Gap fix direction:** Add `ewo_wave3_gate` in `confluence.py` or a momentum
confirmation gate in `automation.py`: wave 3 label is marked CONFIRM only if the
EWO at the wave 3 peak exceeds all other EWO readings in the pattern window.

### G3 — No Structural Invalidation Level per Count (High)

Our engine outputs a ranked list of candidate counts with confidence scores, but does
not associate each count with its structural invalidation level (the specific price
that makes the count impossible). Professionals compute this level for every active
count and use it as a stop anchor and as the condition for switching to the alternate.
Our `ReversalEvent` has an `invalidation` field, but it is computed from the wave
origin price, not from the structural rule (e.g., "below the low of wave 2 = wave 3
label invalid").

**Gap fix direction:** For each labeled count, compute and attach the structural
invalidation price. For an impulse in wave 3: invalidation = below end of wave 2.
For wave 4: invalidation = below top of wave 1. For wave 5: invalidation = below end
of wave 4. Attach to the `WaveCount` output and surface in the reversal zone score.

### G4 — No Alternate-Count Infrastructure with Explicit Invalidation Triggers (Medium)

The engine outputs ranked candidates, but the ranking changes silently as new bars
arrive. There is no "if price breaks $X, promote alternate count B to preferred" logic.
Professional EW service infrastructure explicitly tracks alternate counts with their
invalidation conditions and triggers a count switch when a level is breached.

**Gap fix direction:** After `label_and_validate` produces a candidate list, store
the top-2 candidates with their respective invalidation levels. In the backtest/real-
time loop, monitor whether each candidate's invalidation is breached; if yes, drop
that candidate and promote the next.

### G5 — Log-Price Measurement Not Used (Critical — Being Fixed)

Already documented in audit root cause R1. Professionals use log-price for all
Fibonacci and proportion calculations on instruments spanning more than ~2×. Our
engine uses linear price. This single error makes all Fibonacci quality scores
unreliable on AVGO and MRVL and is the root cause of "everything corrective."
The fix plan (Phase 1, R1) is documented in `00_MISTAKES_AND_FIXES.md` and must
be implemented before any other structural improvement is meaningful.
