## 2. EWF Theory — Topic by Topic

### 2.1 Theoretical Foundation and Modern Adaptation

EWF adopts the Elliott Wave Principle as published by R.N. Elliott (1938) and
formalized by Frost & Prechter. They define its core premise identically:

> "Movement in the direction of the trend unfolds in 5 waves (called motive wave)
> while any correction against the trend is in three waves (called corrective wave)."

Source: [Elliott Wave Theory | Elliott Wave Rules, Guidelines & Structures](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliott-wave-theory/)

**Key modern divergence:** EWF explicitly states that in today's markets, trends do
NOT have to unfold in 5 waves. A trend can complete in 3 waves. This is their
single largest departure from classic Frost & Prechter dogma. They use the term
"motive sequence" (not "motive wave") precisely to avoid implying that 5 subdivisions
are mandatory for a directional move.

> "In today's market, 5 waves moves still happen, but trends do not have to be in 5
> waves and trends can unfold in 3 waves."

Source: [The Elliott Wave Theory and High Frequency Trading](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/the-elliott-wave-theory-and-high-frequency-trading/)

> "The reality is that 90% of the time, the market moves in 3 waves regardless of
> whether it is correcting or trending."

Source: [Elliott Wave Principle: Now and Then](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/elliott-wave-principle-now-and-then-2/)

They attribute this to high-frequency/algorithmic trading, which has reinforced
three-wave structures as the dominant price-delivery mechanism.

### 2.2 The Three Inviolable Rules

EWF restates the canonical three rules verbatim:

1. **Wave 2 must never move beyond the starting point of Wave 1.**
2. **Among Waves 1, 3, and 5, Wave 3 can never be the shortest wave.**
3. **Wave 4 must stay outside the price territory of Wave 1.**

These are called "rules" (as opposed to "guidelines") and are described as
unbreakable. A violation forces a recount.

Source: [Never break these 3 rules](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/never-break-3-rules-successful-elliott-wave-trader/)
Source: [Elliott Wave Theory overview](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliott-wave-theory/)

Note: Rule 3 (wave 4 / wave 1 no-overlap) carries a specific exception: **when the
larger-degree wave is a wave 3, the sub-waves ((i)) and ((iv)) cannot overlap**. In
diagonals (wave 1 or wave 5), overlap between waves 1 and 4 is permitted and common.

Source: [Can Wave 4 overlap with Wave 1?](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/can-wave-4-overlap-wave-1/)

### 2.4 Impulse Wave (Motive, 5-Wave Structure)

#### Structure
Waves 1, 3, 5 are motive (impulse or diagonal); waves 2 and 4 are corrective.
Sub-count: 5-3-5-3-5 for a standard impulse.

#### Extensions
- 90% of extensions occur in Wave 3 (most common).
- Wave 1 extension: Wave 5 must be shorter than Wave 3 (else count is wrong).
- Wave 5 extension: Wave 3 must be longer than Wave 1 (else count is wrong).
- A standard 5-wave impulse contains 5 swings; when Wave 3 extends, the full
  structure expands to 9 swings (13 if both Wave 3 and Wave 5 extend), fitting
  the motive-sequence table 5–9–13–17–21.

Source: [Elliott Wave Extensions in a 5-Wave Move](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/elliott-wave-extensions/)

#### Truncation
EWF explicitly states they **do not label truncated fifths**, because "there is
usually a better option in the Elliott Wave count." This is a practical stance
against forcing the truncation label when a better alternative exists.

Source: [Fifth Wave Trading: Elliott Wave Target Strategies](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/elliott-wave-fifth-wave-target/)

#### Alternation (Guideline)
If Wave 2 is a sharp correction (e.g., zigzag), Wave 4 tends to be sideways (e.g.,
flat, triangle, or combination). The converse also applies. This is a guideline, not
a rule — failing alternation does not invalidate the count.

A standalone triangle **cannot appear** as Wave 2; it is only valid in Wave 4, Wave B,
or Wave X (and occasionally Wave Y).

Source: [Elliott Wave Theory overview](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliott-wave-theory/)

#### Wave Personality Summary (per EWF)

| Wave | Character |
|---|---|
| 1 | Rarely obvious at inception; news still negative; often mistaken for a correction |
| 2 | Sharp, creates doubt; can never exceed start of Wave 1 |
| 3 | Strongest and longest; rapid price move; news turns positive |
| 4 | Clearly corrective; sideways; retraces ≤ 38.2% of Wave 3 typically |
| 5 | Universally bullish sentiment; average investors pile in; RSI divergence forms |

Source: [Elliott Wave personality and characteristics](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/elliott-wave-personality-characheristics/)
Source: [Elliott Wave Five-Wave Structure Explained](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/trading/elliott-wave-five-wave-structure/)

### 2.5 Fibonacci Relationships (EWF's Specific Targets)

EWF's published Fibonacci grid:

**Wave 2 retracement of Wave 1:** 50%, 61.8%, 76.4%, or 85.4%

**Wave 3 extension of Wave 1:** 161.8%, 261.8%, or 323.6%

**Wave 4 retracement of Wave 3:** 14.6%, 23.6%, or 38.2%

**Wave 5:**
- Equal to Wave 1 in points/pips (equality guideline), OR
- 61.8% of the combined length of Waves 1+3, measured from the Wave 4 low, OR
- Inverse 1.236–1.618% extension of the Wave 4 depth measured from the Wave 3 high

### 2.6 Corrective Waves: Zigzag (5-3-5)

**Structure:** A-B-C where A = 5 waves, B = 3 waves, C = 5 waves. Internal count: 5-3-5.

**Key characteristic:** A sharp counter-trend move that retraces a large portion of the
previous impulse. Wave A and Wave C can be standard impulses or diagonals.

**Failure signal:** If the C wave of a zigzag completes but the market does not then
resume the prior trend, a double correction (WXY) is likely forming. EWF identifies
this early by watching whether the market forms a 3-wave sequence prior to a resumption
attempt: if it does, and then breaks below wave A's low (in a bear zigzag), a double
three is in progress.

> "If the third swing extends only to 100% of the first swing, then it's an ABC zigzag.
> If the third swing extends to at least 161.8% of the first swing, then the odds
> increase that we are in an impulsive structure."

Source: [How to spot when a Zig Zag fails](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/how-to-spot-when-a-zig-zag-fails-and-the-corrective-wave-needs-more-time/)
Source: [Elliott Wave Corrective Waves](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/trading/elliott-wave-corrective-waves/)
Source: [Zigzag Elliott Wave Structure Video](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/zigzag-elliott-wave-structure-video/)

### 2.7 Corrective Waves: Flat (3-3-5)

**Structure:** A-B-C where A = 3 waves, B = 3 waves, C = 5 waves. The 3-3-5
internal distinguishes flat from zigzag (5-3-5).

EWF describes three types:

#### Regular Flat
- Wave B terminates near the start of Wave A (approximately at the same level).
- Wave C terminates slightly beyond the end of Wave A.

#### Expanded (Irregular) Flat
- Wave B exceeds the origin of Wave A (goes beyond 100% of Wave A).
- Wave C exceeds the terminus of Wave A (extends beyond 100%).
- Wave C Fibonacci target: 1.236 extension of Wave A relative to Wave B. Can reach
  1.618 or (rarely) 2.618.

#### Running Flat
- Wave B exceeds the origin of Wave A (like expanded).
- Wave C does **not** reach the terminus of Wave A — it falls short.
- This is the key distinction from expanded flat: the "run" never completes the
  correction price-wise, yet the correction is over.

Source: [Understanding the Three Types of Elliott Wave Flat Corrections](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/three-types-of-elliott-wave-flats-2/)
Source: [Elliott Wave Expanded Flat correction](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/elliott-wave-expand-flat-correction/)
Source: [How to Measure Fibonacci Extensions in Elliott Wave Flat Corrections](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/measure-fibonacci-extensions-elliott-wave-flat-corrections/)

**Fibonacci measurement for flats:** EWF sets measurement levels at 61.8%, 100%,
123.6%, and 161.8% extensions to project Wave C targets.

### 2.8 Triangles (3-3-3-3-3)

**General rules:**
- Five legs labeled A-B-C-D-E, each subdividing in 3 waves → 3-3-3-3-3.
- Associated with decreasing volume and volatility.
- Valid positions: **Wave 4** (in an impulse), **Wave B** (in a zigzag), **Wave X**
  (connector in double/triple three), and **Wave Y** (final leg of a WXY, marking end
  of correction). **Never Wave 2.**
- After Wave E completes, the market "thrusts" out of the triangle in the direction
  of the prior actionary wave (the wave that preceded the triangle). Target for the
  thrust: measure the widest portion of the triangle and project from Wave E.

Source: [Elliott Wave Triangle Structure](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/elliott-wave-triangle-structure/)
Source: [Elliott Wave Triangle Patterns: Ascending, Descending & Contracting](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/trading/elliott-wave-triangle-patterns-ascending-descending-contracting/)

#### Four Triangle Types (EWF's taxonomy)

**1. Contracting (Symmetrical) Triangle:** Both trendlines converge. Most common.

**2. Ascending Triangle:** Flat upper trendline, rising lower trendline.

**3. Descending Triangle:** Flat lower trendline, descending upper trendline.

**4. Expanding Triangle:** Both trendlines diverge. Rare. Builds pressure before
a breakout. Can appear in Wave 4 or B/X positions.

Source: [Elliott Wave Corrective Waves](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/trading/elliott-wave-corrective-waves/)
Source: [AUBANK Expanding Triangle](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/stock-market/aubank-elliott-wave-analysis-expanding-triangle-bullish-outlook/)

#### Running Triangle (dedicated EWF topic)

EWF gives running triangles their own articles, treating them as an important and
distinct sub-type:

> "It is quite common, particularly in contracting triangles, for wave b to exceed
> the start of wave a in what may be termed a running triangle. The difference with
> running triangles is that wave B forms a new price extreme above the origin of the
> triangle."

The running triangle looks deceptively like a new impulse beginning (Wave B breaking
out to a new extreme), which "can trick a handful of traders thinking we have formed
a new impulse." EWF notes this is how running triangles are most often misidentified.
Wave B exceeds the origin of wave A; the remaining legs (C, D, E) still form a
converging structure.

Source: [Running Triangle and how they are different to regular Triangles](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/running-triangle-and-how-they-are-different-to-regular-triangles/)
Source: [Elliott Wave Theory: Running Triangle](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/elliott-wave-theory-running-triangle-2/)
Source: [Elliott Wave Theory Structure: A Running Triangle](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/elliott-wave-theory-structure-a-running-triangle/)

### 2.9 Diagonals: Leading and Ending

#### Ending Diagonal (Wave 5 or Wave C)
- 5-wave structure, labeled 1-2-3-4-5.
- **Internal count: 3-3-3-3-3** (each sub-wave is corrective).
- Wave 4 **overlaps** Wave 1's price territory (distinguishing feature vs standard impulse).
- Appears as Wave 5 or as Wave C in a zigzag.
- Signals: the current trend is almost over; a sharp reversal typically follows.
- Visual appearance: wedge shape with converging trendlines (contracting version).

> "An ending diagonal C wave or 5th wave commonly shows an obvious wedge shape
> with an overlapping wave 1 and wave 4."

Source: [Ending Diagonal in Elliott Wave – Pattern & Trading Guide](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/elliott-wave-theory-ending-diagonal/)
Source: [Elliott Wave Diagonal Patterns Explained](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/trading/leading-and-ending-diagonals/)

#### Leading Diagonal (Wave 1 or Wave A)
- 5-wave structure, labeled 1-2-3-4-5.
- **Internal count: 5-3-5-3-5 OR 3-3-3-3-3** (EWF explicitly allows both, unlike
  some analysts who restrict the leading diagonal to 5-3-5-3-5 only).
- Wave 4 usually overlaps Wave 1, but EWF notes this is **not mandatory** for either
  diagonal type.
- Appears as Wave 1 (when the opposing trend is still strong) or as Wave A.
- Signals: trend continuation after correction.

> "Leading Diagonals present themselves in wave 1 instead of wave 5. This happens if
> the opposing trend is still strong, hence why you will witness wave 4 overlapping
> into wave 1's territory."

Source: [Introduction to Diagonals - Part 1](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/introduction-diagonals/)
Source: [Introduction to Diagonals - Part 2](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/introduction-diagonals-part-2/)

**Critical overlap rule clarification:** There is only one case where wave 1/4 overlap
is **impossible** in diagonals: when the larger-degree wave completing is a Wave 3
(because in that case sub-waves ((i)) and ((iv)) cannot overlap — a corollary of the
main impulse no-overlap rule).

Source: [Can Wave 4 overlap with Wave 1?](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/can-wave-4-overlap-wave-1/)

### 2.10 ABC vs WXY — The Critical EWF Distinction

This is one of EWF's most-emphasized teaching points and directly relevant to correct
automated labeling.

**ABC (Zigzag) — use only when:**
- Wave A = 5 sub-waves
- Wave B = 3 sub-waves
- Wave C = 5 sub-waves
- Internal: 5-3-5

**WXY (Double Three) — use when:**
- Wave W = 3 sub-waves (any corrective structure)
- Wave X = 3 sub-waves (connector; can be any corrective pattern including triangle)
- Wave Y = 3 sub-waves
- Internal: 3-3-3

> "The difference between the two is in the internal subdivision of the first leg and
> third leg. In ABC, the subdivision of wave A is in 5 waves and the subdivision of
> wave C is in 5 waves. W and Y of the double-three contain 3 subwaves."

> "Traders tend to be familiar only with ABC and label every 3 waves move as ABC. In
> reality, unless the subdivision of the first leg and third leg is in 5 waves, it's
> wrong to label every 3 waves move as an ABC."

> "WXY (Double Three) pattern is more common than ABC (Zig-Zag)."

Source: [ABC and WXY: difference between both structure](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/difference-wxy-abc-structure/)
Source: [Why Double Three WXY is A Better Structure to Trade Than Zigzag ABC](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/why-double-three-wxy-is-a-better-structure-to-trade-than-zigzag-abc/)

**Why WXY is safer to trade than ABC (EWF's key argument):**

When the first leg (W) is in 3 waves, the analyst knows it cannot be a Wave 1 of an
impulse (since Wave 1 is in 5 waves). If a trader buys at the Wave Y low (100%
extension of Wave W), the probability that the decline is actually a Wave 3 (impulse)
is reduced. With an ABC zigzag, because Wave A is in 5 waves, there is always
ambiguity about whether what looks like Wave A is actually Wave 1 of a new impulse —
the trader risks buying into a Wave 3 decline.

Source: [Why Double Three WXY is A Better Structure to Trade Than Zigzag ABC](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/why-double-three-wxy-is-a-better-structure-to-trade-than-zigzag-abc/)

### 2.11 Double Three (WXY) and Triple Three (WXYXZ)

#### Double Three (W-X-Y) — 7 swings
- W: any corrective structure (zigzag, flat, double three of smaller degree; NOT a
  standalone triangle as the opening wave W)
- X: connector wave — **any** corrective structure including a standalone triangle
- Y: any corrective structure (zigzag, flat, double three, triple three; can also be
  a triangle if it ends the overall pattern in a Wave 4/B/X/Y position)
- Total: 7 price swings

#### Triple Three (W-X-Y-X-Z) — 11 swings
- W, Y, Z: any corrective structure (zigzag, flat, smaller double/triple three)
- X (both connectors): any corrective structure including standalone triangles
- Total: 11 price swings

Source: [Mastering Triple Three Corrections in Elliott Wave Theory](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/elliott-wave-structure-triple-three-corrections/)
Source: [Understanding Double & Triple Combinations in Market Corrections](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/trading/double-triple-combinations-elliott-wave/)

#### Double Zigzag vs Double Three (EWF clarification)
> "Many Elliott wave practitioners call every WXY structure a double zigzag structure
> when in reality, every WXY structure is not a double zigzag structure."

A double zigzag requires that BOTH W and Y are zigzags (A and C are in 5 waves in
each). A double three is the broader category: W and Y can be any corrective structure.
Every double zigzag IS a double three, but not every double three is a double zigzag.

Source: [Why Double Three WXY is A Better Structure to Trade Than Zigzag ABC](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/why-double-three-wxy-is-a-better-structure-to-trade-than-zigzag-abc/)

### 2.12 Swing-Count Sequences (EWF's Distinctive Framework)

This is EWF's most distinctive methodological innovation — a swing-counting system
that operates independently of (but consistent with) classical wave labeling.

#### Motive Sequence: 5–9–13–17–21 swings
- If the swing count is one of these numbers, the trend is **incomplete** and expected
  to extend.
- A standard 5-wave impulse (no extensions) = 5 swings.
- A 5-wave impulse with one extension (e.g., Wave 3 extends) = 9 swings.
- A 5-wave impulse with two extensions = 13 swings.
- Higher counts (17, 21) arise from further nesting.

#### Corrective Sequence: 3–7–11–15–19 swings
- 3 swings: simple correction (zigzag or WXY single degree).
- 7 swings: double three (WXY).
- 11 swings: triple three (WXYXZ).

Source: [Elliott Wave Structures & Swing Sequence](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliott-wave-structures-and-swing-sequence/)
Source: [Trading Edge through Swings Sequences](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/trading-edge-through-swing-sequences/)

#### Incomplete Sequence as Trading Edge

If 5 swings up are visible but the middle correction is too large to qualify as
Wave 4 of an impulse, EWF treats this as "5 swings in a motive sequence" =
incomplete. The 6th swing (a pullback) becomes a buy opportunity to trade the
7th swing higher — completing the motive sequence at 7 swings.

> "When an incomplete swings sequence is spotted on charts, a Bullish Sequence or
> Bearish Sequence stamp is placed on the chart to let clients know they should be
> looking to buy or sell that particular instrument in 3, 7 or 11 swings."

Source: [Trading Right Side using Elliott Wave Theory, Cycles and Sequences](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/trading-right-side-using-elliott-wave-theory-cycles-and-sequences/)

### 2.13 The "Right Side" Concept

EWF uses a proprietary "Right Side" tag on all charts — a directional stamp
(Green = Bullish, Red = Bearish) that tells subscribers whether the larger degree
trend is up or down.

> "Traders who trade the right side will not buy or sell into bounces, and
> consequently will eliminate a lot of bad trades in between."

The Right Side tag is derived from the swing sequence count. If the instrument shows
an incomplete motive sequence (3, 5, 7, 9, or 11 swings that fit a motive count),
the Right Side is stamped in the direction of the dominant trend. Traders are then
instructed to only buy dips (bullish stamp) or only sell rallies (bearish stamp).

Source: [The Right side in Elliott wave](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/right-side-elliott-wave/)
Source: [Trading Elliott Wave Charts with the Right Side Tag and Blue Boxes](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/trading-elliott-wave-chart-right-side-tag/)

### 2.14 Blue Box (High-Frequency Inflection Area)

The "Blue Box" is EWF's proprietary entry-zone tool:

- **Definition:** A price range defined by the 100%–161.8% Fibonacci extension of
  the corrective swing, computed in 3-, 7-, or 11-swing corrective sequences.
- **Claim:** 85% success rate at producing at least a 3-wave reaction.
- **Name:** Called "no enemy areas" because "both buyers and sellers agree in the
  blue box in the direction of the next move for at least 3 waves."
- **Trading use:** Entry in the direction of the Right Side stamp. Stop below the
  lower end of the Blue Box. Risk 2% of capital maximum.
- **Exit:** Take partial off immediately to make position risk-free; allow rest to
  run to next structural target.

### 2.15 RSI Confirmation of Impulse Waves

EWF integrates RSI (Relative Strength Index) as a structural confirmation tool — not
just an overbought/oversold filter. Their specific rules:

- **Five-wave impulse must show three RSI divergences:**
  1. At wave (v) of Wave 1 (momentum fails to exceed (iii) of 1).
  2. At wave (v) of Wave 3 (momentum fails to exceed (iii) of 3).
  3. At Wave 5 itself (price makes higher high, RSI makes lower high).

- **Impulse structures should end with RSI divergence.**
- **Corrective structures should end WITHOUT RSI divergence.**
- If an instrument shows RSI divergence in what is labeled Wave 5, and then erases
  that divergence (RSI makes a new high), EWF switches the count away from Wave 5.

> "Elliott didn't have the luxury to look at RSI or CCI to gather crucial information
> but Elliott Wave Forecast does and considers it imperative to make use of the tools
> available."

Source: [How Momentum Indicator (RSI) is Used with Elliott Wave](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/how-momentum-indicator-is-used-with-elliott-wave/)
Source: [Using Market Correlation and RSI in the new EWF theory](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/the-new-elliott-wave-theory-using-market-correlation-and-rsi-in-elliott-wave/)

### 2.16 Market Correlation Technique ("Elliott Wave 2.0")

EWF describes a technique to reduce subjectivity when a market's wave structure is
ambiguous:

1. Find the "contrarian" — an instrument in the same asset class that has the clearest
   wave structure.
2. Use that structure as a template, transferred (via correlation) to the unclear
   instrument.

> "They look at the overall market and find the contrarian, that is an instrument with
> the clearest structure, and use market correlation to apply the structure of the
> contrarian to another correlated instrument that doesn't have a clear structure."

EWF also uses a proprietary pivot system combining RSI, CCI, and Stochastic RSI to
determine cycle position independent of wave labeling — a forward-looking check on
whether a cycle has ended.

Source: [Elliott Wave Theory 2.0: Market Correlation secrets for better Forecasting](https://elliottwave-forecast.com/elliottwave/elliott-wave-theory-market-correlation/)

### 2.17 HFT / Algorithmic Market Adaptation

EWF explicitly frames their methodology as an adaptation of classical Elliott Wave to
high-frequency, algorithmic markets:

> "Algorithms respond to the underlying market code embedded in price behavior, and
> the theory adapts to the presence of machines rather than machines consciously
> applying the theory."

They argue the Elliott Wave "market code" predates HFT and that machines execute
against the same fractal price structure Elliott described — producing cleaner
three-wave corrections (and 90% three-wave structure overall) because algorithms

### 2.18 Pattern Recognition: Tips and Common Mistakes

**Common mistakes EWF identifies:**

1. Labeling every 3-wave move as ABC instead of verifying whether the first and
   third legs are internally in 5 or 3 waves.
2. Relying on only one timeframe.
3. Emotional attachment to a single count; failing to maintain alternate scenarios.
4. Forcing patterns onto charts rather than letting structure emerge.
5. Using Fibonacci retracements incorrectly or ignoring market structure.
6. Attempting to label truncated fifths when better alternatives exist.

**Practical approach:**
- Start analysis from the monthly timeframe and work down.
- Identify the clearest structural highs and lows before attempting wave labels.
- Verify internal subdivisions before committing to ABC vs WXY distinction.
- Combine wave count with RSI divergence analysis and Fibonacci targets.
- Define clear invalidation levels for each count.

## 3. What EWF Adds or Differs vs Existing Docs (Frost & Prechter / Neely)

The existing docs in this repo (`01_elliott_wave.md`, `02_neowave_neely.md`,
`06_diagonals_and_patterns.md`) cover the canonical Frost & Prechter ruleset and
Neely's NeoWave bottom-up construction. Here is what EWF adds or where they diverge:

### 3.1 Trends in 3 Waves (Major Departure from F&P)

**F&P position:** Motive moves are *always* in 5 waves. Three-wave moves are always
corrective by definition.

**EWF position:** Trends can and do unfold in 3 waves in modern algorithmic markets.
The term "motive sequence" (not "motive wave") is used deliberately to avoid the 5-wave
assumption. This is the single largest theoretical departure.

**Impact on the engine:** Our engine in `wavelib/rules.py` currently enforces the
F&P assumption (5-wave impulse or diagonal for motive). If EWF's 3-wave-trend
observation has empirical validity, the engine's classification of every 3-wave move
as corrective may produce false negatives on genuine impulse continuations.

### 3.2 ABC vs WXY Labeling Discipline

**F&P / existing docs:** The ABC label is used loosely for all simple three-wave
corrections. Internal subdivision check (5-3-5 vs 3-3-3) is discussed but not always
enforced.

**EWF position:** ABC must be reserved for 5-3-5; every other 3-leg correction is
WXY. This is stricter and more operationally useful because it encodes the internal
count in the label itself.

**Impact on engine:** Our `rules.py` `validate_zigzag()` does enforce 5-3-5 internally.
However, the WXY validator (double three) should explicitly reject the ABC label when
the first leg is in 3 waves — surfacing the correct label in the `RuleResult.detail`.

### 3.3 Truncation — EWF's Avoidance Stance

**F&P:** Truncated fifths are a recognized, legitimate pattern with specific trading
implications.

**EWF:** They do not label truncated fifths, preferring to find alternative counts.
This reduces false positives on incomplete structures but may miss genuine truncations
after extended Wave 3s.

**Impact on engine:** Our engine currently does not force a truncation label. This
aligns with EWF's preference but for different reasons (we flag it as WARN rather
than dismissing entirely).

### 3.4 Running Triangle — EWF's Dedicated Treatment

**F&P:** Running triangles are briefly described but not highlighted.

**EWF:** Running triangles get multiple dedicated articles. The key diagnostic: Wave B
makes a new price extreme beyond the origin of the triangle (looks like a new impulse).
This is a significant misidentification risk.

**Impact on engine:** `validate_triangle()` in `rules.py` should include an explicit
running-triangle check: if Wave B exceeds the triangle's origin (point before Wave A),
flag as running triangle variant.

### 3.5 Swing-Count Sequences (No Equivalent in F&P or Neely)

**F&P / Neely:** Wave counts by pattern, not by raw swing counts.

**EWF:** The 5-9-13-17-21 / 3-7-11-15-19 swing-count table is an independent layer
that runs parallel to pattern labeling. It provides a count-independent confirmation
of whether a move is complete or incomplete.

**Impact on engine:** This is a genuinely additive concept. Our `automation.py`
`label_and_validate()` generates candidate counts but does not count raw swings and
map them to the motive/corrective sequence table. Adding this as a separate scoring
dimension could reduce false-completion calls.

### 3.6 RSI Divergence as Structural Rule (Not Just Indicator)

**F&P / existing docs:** RSI divergence is a confirmation tool, not a structural rule.

**EWF:** Three RSI divergences are *required* for a valid 5-wave impulse. Their
absence causes EWF to abandon the count. This elevates RSI from a guideline to a
near-rule.

**Impact on engine:** Our `confluence.py` uses RSI divergence as one of 7 strands.
EWF's stricter approach (3 internal divergences within an impulse) could be encoded
in `rules.py` as a WARN-level check on the impulse validator.

### 3.7 Leading Diagonal: Both 5-3-5-3-5 and 3-3-3-3-3 Allowed

**F&P:** Leading diagonals are 5-3-5-3-5.

**Neely:** Leading diagonals have 5-wave internal (trending impulse sub-types).

**EWF:** Leading diagonals can be either 5-3-5-3-5 OR 3-3-3-3-3.

**Impact on engine:** Our `06_diagonals_and_patterns.md` already covers contracting vs
expanding variants. The 3-3-3-3-3 leading diagonal option should be noted as a valid
sub-type in `validate_leading_diagonal()`.

### 3.8 Wave 4/Wave 1 Overlap: EWF's Nuanced Rule

EWF clarifies: overlap is NOT mandatory in either diagonal type (it "may or may not
happen"). The only case where overlap is truly forbidden is when the larger-degree
structure is Wave 3 of an impulse (where sub-wave ((iv)) cannot enter ((i)) territory).

**Existing docs:** The no-overlap rule for impulses is stated absolutely. EWF's
nuance is that the rule applies specifically to impulse sub-waves, not to diagonals
generally.

### 3.9 Wave X Connector Can Be Any Corrective — Including Standalone Triangle

**F&P / existing docs:** Wave X is a corrective connector but detailed constraints
on what it can be are not always specified.

**EWF specific rule:** Wave X CAN be a standalone triangle (whereas Wave W in a double
three CANNOT be a standalone triangle). This is an actionable discriminator for
automatic labeling.

## 4. Concepts Potentially Useful for the Ewace_2026 Engine

### 4.1 Swing-Count Sequence Table as Independent Validity Check

The 5/9/13/17/21 motive and 3/7/11/15/19 corrective tables could be implemented
as a secondary scoring pass in `automation.py`. After `label_and_validate()` produces
candidate counts, a `check_swing_sequence(pivots)` function counts raw swings (pivot
alternations) and checks whether the total matches a motive or corrective number.
Counts where the swing total does not fit either sequence would receive a lower
confidence score. This is deterministic, causal (all pivots are historical), and
requires no human input.

**Implementation note (causal):** Swing count at bar `t` uses only pivots ≤ `t`.
No future-bar lookback needed — fully compatible with the D-013 requirement.

### 4.2 ABC vs WXY Labeling Enforcement

Enforce the EWF rule: use `ABC` label only if the first and third legs each internally
count to 5 waves. Otherwise emit `WXY`. This is already the structural intent in the
codebase but could be made an explicit discriminator gate that surfaces in
`RuleResult.detail` as `"first_leg_internal=3 → label=WXY, not ABC"`.

### 4.3 Running Triangle Explicit Detection

Add a `is_running_triangle` flag in the triangle validator: true when Wave B exceeds
the starting point of Wave A. The current validator may not surface this as a distinct
variant. It matters because the post-triangle thrust direction and size calculations
differ.

### 4.4 RSI Divergence as Impulse Structural Check (WARN Level)

In `validate_impulse()`, after confirming the three hard rules, add a WARN if RSI
does not show divergence at Wave 5. A stronger check (optional, REF-level) would
check for three internal divergences. This would partially implement EWF's
objective confirmation layer without overriding structural rules.

### 4.5 Zigzag-to-Double-Three Transition Signal

EWF's observation: if a zigzag's Wave C completes but then the market produces a
3-wave move before attempting a resumption — and that 3-wave move breaks Wave A's
low — a double three is in progress. This "failed zigzag" → double three detection
could be encoded as a state-machine transition in `automation.py`:
`ZZ_COMPLETE → observe_extension → if_break_then_WXY`.
