## p32: ESSENTIAL RULES FOR ZIGZAG (1 of 2)
- RULES:
  - Zigzag internally comprises of 3 segments or waves, structured as 5-3-5.
  - B should be LESS than 61.8% of A.
  - C should move beyond the end of A.
  - C usually relates to A price-wise, or to the total of A+B time-wise; relation is usually equality or a Fibonacci ratio.
  - Wave B should take same or MORE time than wave A.
- METHOD/PROCESS: Inset diagram (Neely-style "ZigZags 5-3-5's") shows two zigzag variants labeled a:5, b:3, c:5 each drawn inside parallel channel lines (channeling the zigzag is part of identification).

## p33: ESSENTIAL RULES FOR ZIGZAG (2 of 2)
- RULES:
  - B could be even 1% of A (i.e., no minimum retracement for B in a zigzag; only the <61.8% max applies).
  - 0-B line should be broken in EQUAL OR LESSER time period than that of C — **Neely's Rule of Confirmation**.
  - Only 3 (out of possible 4) touch points should touch the parallel trend lines — **Neely's Touch Point rule**.
  - If C ends ON the parallel channel, then development of a complex corrective involving an "x" wave is possible (pattern may not be over).
- METHOD/PROCESS: Diagram shows an impulse 1-2-3-4-5 followed by a-b-c decline; the "0-b line" is drawn from the start of the correction (point 0 = end of wave 5) through the b high, extended right. Confirmation = price breaking that 0-b line after c, within time <= time of C. So after labeling a-b-c: DRAW the 0-B line, then WATCH the time taken to break it vs time of C.

## p34: ESSENTIAL RULES FOR A FLAT (1 of 2)
- RULES:
  - Flat internally comprises of 3 segments or waves, structured as 3-3-5.
  - B should be MORE than 61.8% of A (this is the flat/zigzag discriminator vs p32).
  - C usually relates to A price-wise, or to the total of A+B time-wise; usually equality or Fibonacci ratio.
  - 0-B line should be broken in equal or lesser time period than that of C — **Neely's Rule of Confirmation**.
  - Wave B should take same or more time than wave A.
- EXAMPLES: Inset shows the flat variations taxonomy with labels: Running, C Failure, B Failure, Irregular Failure, Irregular, Elongated — each drawn a:3, b:3, c:5, with horizontal reference lines at A's start/end levels ("Flat Upward Correction" family).

## p35: ESSENTIAL RULES FOR A FLAT (2 of 2)
- RULES:
  - Only 3 (out of possible 4) touch points should touch the parallel trend lines — **Neely's Touch Point rule**.
  - If C ends on the parallel channel, then development of complex corrective involving an "x" wave is possible.
- METHOD/PROCESS: Same channel-discipline as zigzag: draw parallel lines around the flat; count touch points; a 4th touch / C ending on the channel = warning of complexity extension.

## p36: ESSENTIAL RULES FOR A TRIANGLE (1 of 2)
- RULES:
  - Triangle internally comprises of 5 segments, structured as 3-3-3-3-3.
  - Each segment is a COMPLETE corrective.
  - Triangle can drift upward or downward.
  - A does NOT have to be the largest wave.
  - E MUST be the smallest wave.
- EXAMPLES: Inset reproduces Neely's four-panel chart: "Contracting Limiting Triangles" (Simple / Complex, correcting down trend and up trend, positions labeled "Wave-3 or A", "Wave-4 or B", "Wave-5 or C") and "Contracting Non-Limiting Triangles" (with x-wave, "End of larger combination pattern" arrows) — i.e., limiting triangles occur in wave 4/B positions; non-limiting triangles end combinations.

## p37: ESSENTIAL RULES FOR A TRIANGLE (2 of 2)
- RULES:
  - At least 3 segments must correct MORE than 50% of the previous segment.
  - Only 4 (out of possible 6) touch points should touch the two opposing trend lines — **Neely's Touch Point Rule** (triangle version).
  - B-D is the BASE LINE and the same should be CLEAN (no violations through it).
  - On the other side: draw the A-C line when C is SHORTER than B.
  - Draw the C-E line on the other side when C is BIGGER than B.
  - Triangle is over when the B-D line gets broken in EQUAL OR LESSER time period than that of E — **Neely's Rule of Confirmation** (triangle version).
- METHOD/PROCESS: Explicit drawing procedure for triangles: (1) always draw B-D trendline first and verify it is clean; (2) choose the opposite line by comparing C vs B size (A-C if C<B, C-E if C>B); (3) confirmation = post-E thrust breaks B-D line in time <= time of wave E.

## p38: 2 STAGE CONFIRMATION
- RULES/METHOD: Pure diagram (Neely's two-stage confirmation of a corrective). An a-b-c correction (down: a low, b high, c lower low) is drawn under a downsloping trendline anchored at the pre-correction top through b. Dashed horizontal lines are drawn at the level of b (upper) and at the level of a (lower). After c, price rallies. Stage 1 (bracket drawn at bottom around c and the time to its right): break of the trendline/first level in comparable time. Stage 2 (bracket drawn at top): price exceeds the b high (arrow at the dashed line through b) — the move beyond b confirms the correction is complete and a new trend is underway. Time brackets drawn on the chart indicate each stage must occur within a time window comparable to the wave it confirms (consistent with Rule of Confirmation on p33/34/37).
- METHOD/PROCESS: After a suspected correction ends: mark level of b and the 0-b/trendline; confirmation is sequential — first the line break, then exceeding b's extreme.

## p39: DOUBLE COMBINATION (1 of 2)
- RULES:
  - Double Zigzag panel: "Incorrect Interpretation" = counting a channeled, overlapping 5-segment decline as impulse (1)-(2)-1-2-3-4-5; "Correct Interpretation" = a:5, b:3, c:5 — x:3 — a:5, b:3, c:5 (zigzag - x wave - zigzag). Key visual cue: the whole move fits in a parallel channel and overlaps — impulses don't channel (see p45).
  - Double Combination ending with a Triangle: incorrect = (1)(2)1-2-3-4-5(3) then a triangle as wave 4; correct = a:3, b:3, c:5 (flat/zigzag) — x:3 — triangle a:3,b:3,c:3,d:3,e:5(ends combination). A combination = corrective + x + corrective, second ending in a triangle.
- METHOD/PROCESS: The taught discrimination step: when a "trend" is channeled and internally 3s, relabel as double zigzag/combination, not impulse.

## p40: DOUBLE COMBINATION (2 of 2)
- RULES:
  - Double Combination ending with a Flat: incorrect = 1-2-3-4(triangle)-5 with b following; correct = a:5,b:3,c:5 (zigzag), x:3 (the triangle is inside/at x), then a:3,b:3,c:5 flat where final c:5 is a FAILURE (c fails to exceed a) — labeled "c:5 Failure".
  - Double Flat: incorrect = 1-2-3-4-5 channeled decline; correct = a:3,b:3,c:5 — x — a:3,b:3,c:5 (flat-x-flat), all inside a parallel channel.
- METHOD/PROCESS: Again: parallel channel + 3-wave internals ⇒ complex corrective, not impulse.

## p41: TRIPPLE COMBINATION (1 of 3)
- RULES:
  - Triple Zigzag: incorrect = extended impulse (1)(2)(i)(ii)1-2-3-4-5(iii)(iv)(v)(3) inside a channel; correct = a:5,b:3,c:5 - x:3 - a:5,b:3,c:5 - x:3 - a:5,b:3,c:5 (three zigzags joined by two x waves).
  - Triple Combination: incorrect = impulse count with terminal triangle (iv)?; correct = a:5,b:3,c:5 - x:3 - a:5,b:3,c:5 - x:3 - triangle a:3,b:3,c:3,d:3,e:3 (third corrective is a triangle that ends the pattern).
- METHOD/PROCESS: A steadily channeled trend where every advance is a "three" = triple combination; the terminal triangle warns the whole structure is ending.

## p43: TRIPPLE COMBINATION (3 of 3) — Double Three (Running variation)
- RULES:
  - "Double Three Combination (Running variation) — Correct Interpretation": after an up impulse (dashed), a:3 - c:5 form a flat-type correction (bracket at bottom around a:3/c:5), then x:3 rises ABOVE the start of the correction (bracket at top around x:3 — the running characteristic), then b:3 - d:3 - e:5 triangle drifting UP, then strong continuation up (dashed arrow). The x wave making a HIGHER high while the correction is still in progress = "running" — signals strong underlying trend.
  - Right panel: same running double three drawn bigger: a:3 - c:5 (lower bracket), x:3 (upper bracket, higher high), a:3 - b:3 - c:3 sideways, then dashed arrow strongly up.
- METHOD/PROCESS: When a correction keeps making higher lows/higher x-highs above the prior extreme, do NOT count it as reversal — it is a running correction; expect continuation in the prior trend direction with power.

## p44: IMPORTANT APPLICATION PRINCIPLES (1 of 2)
- RULES:
  - Impulse can be upwards or downwards.
  - Corrective can be upwards or downwards, going in the opposite direction to the move it is correcting.
  - **Rule of Similarity & Balance: the smaller of two adjacent waves of the same degree should be at least 1/3rd of the other, price-wise or time-wise** (else they are not the same degree).
- METHOD/PROCESS:
  - Use WAVE CHARTS plotting high and low of the period in chronological order (not close-only, not candles) — this is the chart preparation step.
  - MONO-WAVE is the basic unit of analysis (start analysis by isolating monowaves).
  - Wave analysis has an explanation for each and every part of the chart, for any time frame (no bar may be left unlabeled).

## p45: IMPORTANT APPLICATION PRINCIPLES (2 of 2)
- RULES:
  - Each completed pattern does imply and transfer a specific amount of "Power" to future market action.
  - Corrective usually consumes MORE time than the move it is correcting, EXCEPT in Triangles, Terminals, Diametric and Symmetrical.
  - Corrective is usually properly sub-divided, while Impulse may look like a Mono-Wave.
  - Each impulse and corrective is part of a bigger wave, which is part of a still bigger wave (fractality/degree stacking).
  - **Impulse would usually NOT get channeled into parallel trend lines** (key discriminator: if it channels neatly, suspect corrective/complex).
- METHOD/PROCESS: Practical checks when deciding impulse vs corrective: time consumed, subdivision visibility, and whether the move fits a parallel channel.

- METHOD/PROCESS: After labeling a completed pattern, the NEXT step is to derive the implied extent/behavior of the following move from the pattern class (thrust from limiting triangle, etc.). (Slide is a header/agenda for the implications; the specific magnitudes are in the pattern slides.)

## p47: DIAMETRIC
- RULES:
  - A Diametric formation has 7 legs labeled A-B-C-D-E-F-G, all "threes".
  - "A Diametric Formation generally resembles the shape of a Bow Tie": first half contracts (A-B-C-D converge), second half expands (D-E-F-G diverge) — drawn with a converging pair of lines into D and a diverging pair out of D.
- METHOD/PROCESS: Drawing: line across A-C(-E) tops and line across B-D(-F) bottoms show contraction then expansion around the middle leg D. G is the final leg; its completion ends the pattern.

## p49: ICHIMOKU — introduction (Ultratech Cement example)
- INDICATORS:
  - "Ichimoku cloud indicator is basically an all-in-one indicator: identifies support, resistance, trend direction, entry points, measures momentum."

  - **Trade filter rule: for any trade setup BOTH lines (Tenkan & Kijun) should move together in the SAME direction AND the stock must be trading ABOVE (long) or BELOW (short) the cloud.**

## p50: ICHIMOKU — formulas (settings)
- INDICATORS (exact formulas as printed):
  - Conversion Line (Tenkan-sen) = (9-period high + 9-period low) / 2
  - Base Line (Kijun-sen) = (26-day high + 26-day low) / 2
  - Leading Span A (Senkou Span A) = (Conversion Line + Base Line) / 2 [plotted ahead]
  - Leading Span B (Senkou Span B) = (52-period high + 52-period low) / 2
  - Lagging Span (Chikou Span) = Close plotted 26 periods in the PAST
- RULES: Standard 9/26/52 settings with 26-period displacement are the taught configuration.

## p52: NEUTRAL TRIANGLE
- RULES:
  - Neutral Triangle: 5 legs A-B-C-D-E between two slightly diverging/flat lines; **wave C is the LONGEST** (points far below the A-E line).
  - Printed note: "Just like what you might witness in a 3rd Extension Impulse pattern, the 'extended' c-wave of a Neutral Triangle is usually the longest, most subdivided and most time consuming of the three 'trending' waves (i.e., waves a, b, c)." (Trending waves here = a, c, e; c is the extended one.)
- METHOD/PROCESS: Drawing shows upper line through B-D, lower line through A-E, with C spiking below both — i.e., don't force C inside the lines; a C that breaks the base line but with B-D clean can still be a (neutral) triangle.

## p53: 3rd EXTENSION TERMINAL
- RULES:
  - Terminal (ending diagonal, NeoWave "3rd extension terminal"): labeled 1, 2, x3 (wave 3 extended), 4, 5; wedge formed by line through 1-(x3 area) tops and line through 2-4 bottoms.
  - Wave 4 OVERLAPS wave 1 territory (defining trait of a terminal); wave 3 is the extended leg; wave 5 breaks above the 1-x3 upper line (throw-over) at the end.
- METHOD/PROCESS: Draw the 2-4 line and the 1-3 line: converging wedge with overlap ⇒ terminal impulse ⇒ expect complete retracement of the terminal after wave 5.

## SYNTHESIS p32-62

### The analysis sequence taught in these pages
1. **Prepare the chart**: use wave charts plotting the HIGH and LOW of each period in chronological order (p44); log scale for long horizons (p57-58); the mono-wave is the basic unit — every part of the chart must get a label (p44).
2. **Start at the highest degree and work down**: monthly (p57) → weekly (p56, p58-60) → daily (p61) → hourly (p62). Each lower timeframe count must nest inside the higher one.
3. **Classify each 3-wave or multi-leg unit by measured rules, not by look**: B<61.8% of A ⇒ zigzag (p32); B>61.8% of A ⇒ flat (p34); 5 threes with E smallest and ≥3 legs retracing >50% ⇒ triangle (p36-37); 7 threes bow-tie ⇒ diametric (p47); check the Rule of Similarity & Balance (adjacent same-degree waves ≥1/3 of each other in price or time, p44) before assigning degree.
4. **Draw the confirmation lines immediately after labeling**: 0-B line for zigzags/flats (p33-34); B-D base line (must be clean) plus A-C or C-E line chosen by whether C<B or C>B for triangles (p37); channel every corrective with parallels and count touch points (max 3 of 4 for zigzag/flat, 4 of 6 for triangle — Neely's Touch Point rule).
5. **Confirm before acting — in PRICE and TIME**: pattern is over only when the confirmation line (0-B or B-D) breaks in equal or lesser time than the final wave (C or E) — Neely's Rule of Confirmation (p33, 34, 37); then 2-Stage Confirmation: line break first, then exceeding the b-extreme (p38).
6. **If confirmation fails or C ends on the channel, escalate complexity**: expect an x-wave and relabel as double/triple combination (p35, 39-43); channeled overlapping "trends" of threes are combinations, NOT impulses, because impulses do not channel (p45).
7. **Derive the forward implication from the completed pattern class** (p46): each completed pattern implies the extent of the next move (limiting vs non-limiting triangle, terminal ⇒ full retracement, running correction ⇒ powerful continuation, p43, p53).
8. **Layer time cycles on the count**: 272-week cycle on Sensex monthly (p57), 85-week Hurst cycle on Nifty weekly (p58-60), 55-day cycle on Nifty daily (p61) — reversals are anticipated where the wave count says "final leg" AND a cycle date is due (this combination "anticipated Oct 2024", p59).
9. **Add Ichimoku (9/26/52, displacement 26) as the trade filter/trigger** (p49-51): trade only when Tenkan and Kijun move together in the trade direction AND price is on the correct side of the cloud; re-enter on pullbacks to the lines while the cloud relationship holds (Titan SELL x3, p51).
10. **Execute on the hourly with confluence levels**: wave-equality measurements (e.g., (e)=(c)), Gann price levels, and trendlines define entry/decision zones (p62).

### The 10 most operationally specific rules found (p32-62)
1. Zigzag: B must be LESS than 61.8% of A (can be as little as 1%); C must move beyond the end of A; structure 5-3-5 (p32-33).
2. Flat: B must be MORE than 61.8% of A; structure 3-3-5 (p34).
3. Wave B should take the same or MORE time than wave A (zigzags and flats) (p32, p34).
4. Neely's Rule of Confirmation: the 0-B line must be broken in equal or lesser time than wave C took (zigzag/flat); the B-D line must be broken in equal or lesser time than wave E took (triangle) — otherwise the pattern is not over (p33, 34, 37).
5. Neely's Touch Point rule: only 3 of 4 possible touches on the parallel channel for zigzag/flat; only 4 of 6 on the converging lines for a triangle; and if C ends ON the channel, expect an x-wave complex corrective (p33, 35, 37).
6. Triangle: 3-3-3-3-3; at least 3 segments retrace >50% of the prior segment; E must be the smallest; B-D base line must be clean; draw A-C line if C<B, C-E line if C>B (p36-37).
7. Rule of Similarity & Balance: the smaller of two adjacent same-degree waves must be at least 1/3 of the other, price-wise or time-wise (p44).
8. Impulse vs corrective discriminators: a corrective usually takes MORE time than the move it corrects (except triangles/terminals/diametric/symmetrical); correctives subdivide visibly while impulses can look like mono-waves; impulses do NOT fit parallel channels — a neatly channeled overlapping move is a combination (p45, p39-41).
9. Diametric = 7 legs A-G of threes in a bow-tie (contract then expand); Neutral triangle: C is longest/most subdivided; Extracting triangle: each decline smaller, each rally larger, D always larger than C; Terminal: wave 4 overlaps wave 1 in a wedge with extended 3rd (p47, 52-55).
10. Ichimoku trade filter (settings 9/26/52, Kijun=26-day midpoint, spans displaced 26): only take a setup when Tenkan and Kijun point the same way AND price is above (long) / below (short) the cloud; pullbacks to the lines under a bearish cloud are repeat SELLs (p49-51).
